Privacy Policy — PlateAware

Last updated: 2026-06-11

This policy describes what data PlateAware ("the app") collects, stores, and shares. It is written to match the App Store Connect App Privacy nutrition label exactly. If this document and the nutrition label ever disagree, the nutrition label is wrong — let us know at support@plateaware.app.

What the app collects

PlateAware is designed to work without an account and with the minimum data needed to render a public-infrastructure transparency map. Specifically:

  • Anonymous device identifier. On first launch, the app generates a random UUID and stores it in the iOS Keychain. This identifier is not your Apple ID, not your IDFA/IDFV, and is not used for advertising. It is used only to attribute your in-app entitlement, your submitted camera reports, and your PostHog analytics events to the same anonymous installation.
  • Approximate location. While the map screen is open, PlateAware sends an approximate latitude/longitude to our server with each /v1/cameras/nearby or /v1/cameras/bbox request so we can return camera pins near you. The coordinates are used to compute the response and are not stored in our database beyond the request lifetime.
  • Optional background location (premium "Camera Ahead"). If you turn on the premium Camera Ahead proximity-alert feature, the app reads your location in the background to fire a local notification before you reach a known fixed camera. You can turn this off at any time in PlateAware Settings or in iOS Settings. It is off by default and is never requested at launch.
  • User-submitted camera reports. If you tap "Submit a Camera Report," you may submit a camera type, an approximate latitude/longitude (defaulting to your current location only if you enable that toggle), an optional photo, and an optional free-text note. Reports enter a moderation queue and are never published to other users until a moderator verifies them. They are linked to your anonymous device identifier (or to your Sign in with Apple identifier if you have signed in).
  • In-app purchase records. When you start a free trial or purchase a subscription, Apple StoreKit hands us the transaction identifier so we can grant your entitlement. We never see card data, billing address, or your Apple ID password.
  • Sign in with Apple email (optional, premium only). If you choose to sign in with Apple to sync saved routes across devices, Apple provides a private relay email address that we store server-side. We use it only to contact you about account-deletion requests; we do not market to it.
  • PostHog product analytics. PlateAware sends anonymized product-analytics events (screen views, feature taps, paywall views) to PostHog in the United States. Events are attributed to the anonymous device identifier described above. We do not send precise location, do not send report contents, and do not use these events for advertising or cross-app tracking.

What the app stores locally

PlateAware keeps a local SQLite cache of the public camera dataset on your device so the map can render fast and so an offline state pack (premium) keeps working without a network. Your anonymous device identifier is stored in the iOS Keychain. Your saved routes and your in-app entitlement are stored in the app's local container. Photos you attach to a report are held only until upload succeeds, then released.

What the app transmits

PlateAware transmits the following over HTTPS:

  • Approximate latitude/longitude and the anonymous device identifier with each map / route-risk / sync API request to api.platewatch.app (AWS us-east-2, account 139294832887).
  • Camera reports — type, approximate coordinates, optional photo, optional note — to the same API and an associated private S3 bucket.
  • Subscription receipts to Apple StoreKit; the resulting transaction identifier is forwarded to our API for entitlement validation.
  • Anonymized product-analytics events to PostHog (us.i.posthog.com).
  • If you sign in with Apple, Apple's relay email address is shared with our API once at sign-in.

PlateAware does not send data to advertising networks, data brokers, or any party other than the services listed below.

Third-party services

  • Apple StoreKit and Sign in with Apple. Used for subscription purchase, restore, and optional authentication. Apple's privacy policy applies: apple.com/legal/privacy.
  • Amazon Web Services (AWS). Our API runs on AWS Lambda + DynamoDB + S3 in the us-east-2 region. AWS acts as our data processor. Privacy: aws.amazon.com/privacy.
  • PostHog. Anonymized product analytics, US cloud host. PostHog acts as our data processor under its standard DPA. Privacy: posthog.com/privacy.
  • DeFlock community dataset (built on OpenStreetMap). The camera locations PlateAware displays come from the DeFlock community map at maps.deflock.org, which is itself built on OpenStreetMap and distributed under the ODbL. We ingest the dataset on a weekly schedule on our server; the iOS app does not contact DeFlock or OpenStreetMap directly at runtime.

PlateAware does not use any third-party advertising network, attribution network, ad-ID provider, or data broker.

Permissions we request

  • Location — While Using the App. Requested when you open the map so we can center it on roads near you and return nearby cameras. If you decline, the map opens at a default region and you can pan manually.
  • Location — Always. Requested only after you explicitly turn on the premium Camera Ahead proximity alerts in Settings. Used only to fire a local notification before you reach a known fixed camera. You can revoke it any time.
  • Camera. Requested only when you tap "Add Photo" on a camera-report submission form. Used to capture a photo to attach to your report.
  • Photo Library. Requested only when you tap "Choose from Library" on a camera-report submission form. We only access the photo you pick.

PlateAware does not request microphone, contacts, HealthKit, motion, Bluetooth, Face ID, App Tracking Transparency, or remote push notifications. Local notifications are used only for the optional Camera Ahead alerts and require no separate permission beyond iOS notification settings.

Your controls

  • Revoke any permission in iOS Settings → PlateAware at any time.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all locally-stored data.
  • Delete All Data (anonymous users): Settings → Delete All Data wipes the install-trial stamp, onboarding state, and the local entitlement on this device. This is reachable in two taps from the main map view.
  • Delete Account (Sign in with Apple users): Settings → Delete Account clears all PlateAware data on the device and opens Apple's Sign in with Apple manager (https://appleid.apple.com/account/manage) so you can revoke the grant directly via Apple's UI. Full server-side row wipe via authenticated DELETE /v1/account + Apple-side auth/revoke lands in API v1.1. No email round-trip required.
  • Contact us at support@plateaware.app to request any data we hold or to ask a question about this policy.

Children

PlateAware is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has used the app and entered personal data, contact us and we'll delete it.

Changes

We'll update this page when the data we collect changes. The "Last updated" date at the top reflects the most recent change. Material changes (new data types, new third-party services) will also prompt an in-app notice on next launch.
